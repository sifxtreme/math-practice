🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — kid1
1. 10 kites.
Backwards, flipping each step: 7 left after halving → 14. Undo building 4 → 10. Check forwards: 10 + 4 = 14, sell half = 7. ✓
2. 3 kites.
At least one = 12 + 9 − 4 = 17. Then 20 − 17 = 3.
3. 21 and 28. The gaps go up by one each time.
Gaps are 2, 3, 4, 5 — so next gaps are 6 and 7. 15 + 6 = 21, then 21 + 7 = 28.
4. 30 bells.
4 large is double 2 large, so 4 large = 10 small. Each small is 3 bells, so 10 × 3 = 30.
Watch for: Cumulative sheet — the point is that nothing says which puzzle is which. #1 is working backwards, #2 is the overlap circles, #3 is a growing-gap pattern, #4 is swapping one thing for another. Watch whether he recognises the type unprompted; that recognition is the whole difference between Solid and Locked.
4th Grade — kid2
1. Hamza — red — hill · kid1 — blue — beach · kid2 — green — park.
Clue 3 puts Hamza on the hill, so not blue (beach, clue 2), and not green → Hamza, red. Clue 1 rules kid1 off red anyway. Clue 4 keeps kid2 off the beach, so kid2 isn't blue → kid1, blue, beach and kid2, green, leaving kid2 the park.
2. 60 days.
The first number both 15 and 20 divide into: multiples of 20 are 20, 40, 60, and 60 ÷ 15 = 4. So 60 days.
3. Anas broke it.
Careful — this time exactly one lies, so two are telling the truth. If Anas did it: Anas lies, Bilal is honest, Idris is honest — exactly one lie. ✓ If Bilal did it: Bilal and Idris both lie — two. ✗ If Idris did it: Anas and Idris both lie — two. ✗
4. 2 weighings.
Split 3 / 3 / 2. Weigh 1: the two 3s. If one side sinks you have 3 left; if they balance you have the 2 left. Weigh 2: from 3, weigh one against another (sinks = that one, balances = the third); from 2, just weigh them. 2 either way.
Watch for: #3 flips the rule from Tuesday's puzzle: exactly one lies, not exactly one tells the truth. If he answers Bilal, he pattern-matched Tuesday instead of reading the line — which is exactly what a cumulative sheet is built to catch. Worth more as a lesson than as a mark.
