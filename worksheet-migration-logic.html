🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa
1. S, Q, P, R.
Q > P and R < P puts them in order Q, P, R. S > Q goes on the front: S, Q, P, R. Building the chain one clue at a time beats guessing.
2. Tariq — whale. Nadia — goose. Yusra — turtle.
Tariq is given. Nadia is not the turtle and the whale is taken, so Nadia has the goose. Yusra gets what is left. The “not” clue is the one that does the work.
3. 48 geese.
2:00 → 3:00 is 60 minutes = three 20-minute steps. 6 → 12 → 24 → 48. Counting the doublings is the whole puzzle; 6 × 3 = 18 is the trap.
Both tags: boxes 12 and 24. No tag at all: 12 boxes.
Both = multiples of 12 → 12 and 24. Blue (3, 6, 9…) is 8 boxes, red (4, 8, 12…) is 6, minus the 2 counted twice = 12 tagged, so 24 − 12 = 12 untagged.
Watch for: #3 is the one that gets missed — the trap is multiplying by 3 instead of doubling three times. On #4, expect 8 + 6 = 14 tagged; boxes 12 and 24 get counted twice and must come back out. On #2, the “not” clue is easy to skip straight past.
4th Grade — Yusuf
1. Swift, tern, stork, crane, goose.
Swift > tern > stork from the first and third clues; goose is below the stork; the crane sits between goose and stork. Five items, and the “between” clue only makes sense once the others are placed.
No — it proves nothing about Tuesday.
Her statement is only about Monday's birds. A goose on Tuesday is neither proof nor disproof. The one thing that would disprove her: a Monday bird that was not a goose. Accept any answer that separates “all Monday's were geese” from “all geese are tagged on Monday”.
1:55 in the afternoon (13:55) local time.
6:40 + 8 h = 2:40 pm, + 15 min = 2:55 pm bay time. Local clocks are 1 hour behind, so 1:55 pm. Two steps: do the elapsed time first, adjust the zone second.
8 posts.
315 ÷ 45 = 7 gaps, and posts at both ends means 7 + 1 = 8. The classic fencepost error: 7 is the number of gaps, not posts.
15 handshakes.
Each of the 6 teams shakes 5 others = 30, but every handshake was counted from both sides, so 30 ÷ 2 = 15. Or add 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1.
Watch for: #4 and #5 are both double-counting traps in opposite directions — #4 needs a plus one, #5 needs a divide by two. Expect 7 and 30. #2 has no number in it at all and is the most valuable one on the sheet: the reasoning written down is the answer, so a bare “no” is not finished.
