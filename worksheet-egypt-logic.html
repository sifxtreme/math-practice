🔺 Ancient Egypt Logic & Puzzles

Brain-teasers from the Nile, the pyramids, and the tombs
3rd Grade
Name:  kid1
Date:
1.
Three helpers carried water jars up to the pyramid workers: kid1, Bilal, and Zayd. Together they carried 11 jars. Bilal carried 5 jars and Zayd carried 2. How many did kid1 carry? Whose number was in the middle?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The pyramid builders were paid workers, not slaves. Records show they were paid in bread, beer, and grain.
2.
A painted border in a tomb counts up in a pattern: 4, 8, 12, 16, ___, ___ . What are the next two numbers, and what is the rule?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Egyptians wrote numbers as pictures. A coiled rope meant 100, a lotus flower meant 1,000, and a bent finger meant 10,000.
3.
Amina counted 9 carved scarab beetles on a wall. There were 3 more blue ones than green ones. How many were blue, and how many were green? (Hint: try pairs of numbers that add up to 9.)
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The scarab beetle rolls a ball of dung across the ground. Egyptians thought it looked like something rolling the sun across the sky, so it became a symbol of a new day.
4.
Four boats sailed down the Nile: the Falcon, the Lotus, the Ibis, and the Sun. The Falcon arrived 1st. The Lotus arrived last. The Ibis arrived ahead of the Sun. List all four boats in order from 1st to 4th.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The Nile flows north, and the wind blows south. So boats could drift downstream one way and raise a sail to come back — a free ride both directions.
5.
kid1 is making a necklace with one bead — red, blue, or gold — on one cord — leather or linen. How many different necklaces can he make? Try listing them all.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Egyptians made the world's first man-made color, called Egyptian blue, by baking sand, copper, and lime together over 4,000 years ago.

4th Grade
Name:  kid2
Date:
1.
In a board game, a house square earns 5 points, a blank square earns 1 point, and a water square costs 2 points. kid2 lands on 3 house squares, 2 blank squares, and 1 water square. Zaid lands on 1 house square, 7 blank squares, and 2 water squares. How many points does each boy have? Who has more, and by how many?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Senet is one of the oldest board games we know of — Egyptians were playing it more than 5,000 years ago.
2.
Three workers — kid2, Idris, and Hamza — have three jobs at the site: scribe, stone-cutter, and water-carrier. Clue 1: kid2 is not the scribe. Clue 2: Hamza is the water-carrier. Which job does each worker have?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Only a small number of Egyptians could read and write. Becoming a scribe took years of school, and scribes did not have to pay taxes.
3.
A small step pyramid has 5 layers. The bottom layer uses 80 blocks, and each layer above it uses half as many blocks as the layer below. How many blocks are in the top layer? How many blocks are in the whole pyramid?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The Great Pyramid at Giza used more than 2 million stone blocks, and most of them weigh more than a car.
4.
Two brothers copied hieroglyph symbols onto papyrus. Together they copied 26 symbols. kid2 copied 6 more than kid1. How many symbols did each brother copy?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Hieroglyphs went unread for about 1,400 years. The Rosetta Stone finally cracked them, because it carried the same message in three kinds of writing.
5.
A festival has 15 dancers split into equal groups of 5. How many groups are there? Inside one group, each dancer greets every other dancer exactly once. How many greetings happen in that group? (Hint: label them A, B, C, D, E and list the pairs.)
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Egypt's biggest festivals followed the Nile's yearly flood, which left behind dark, rich mud that made the farmland grow.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — reasoning included
Coach
3rd Grade
1. kid1 carried 4 jars; kid1 is the middle.
11 total − 5 (Bilal) − 2 (Zayd) = 4 for kid1. In order: Zayd 2, kid1 4, Bilal 5 → kid1 is in the middle.
2. 20 and 24. Rule: add 4 each time.
4, 8, 12, 16 each go up by 4. 16 + 4 = 20, 20 + 4 = 24.
3. 6 blue, 3 green.
The two numbers add to 9 and differ by 3. Guess & check: 6 + 3 = 9 and 6 − 3 = 3. So 6 blue, 3 green.
4. Falcon, Ibis, Sun, Lotus.
Falcon is 1st and Lotus is 4th. That leaves 2nd and 3rd for Ibis and Sun; Ibis is ahead, so Ibis 2nd, Sun 3rd. Order: Falcon, Ibis, Sun, Lotus.
5. 6 necklaces.
3 bead colors × 2 cords = 6. (red-leather, red-linen, blue-leather, blue-linen, gold-leather, gold-linen.)
4th Grade
1. kid2 15, Zaid 8. kid2 has 7 more.
kid2: (3 × 5) + (2 × 1) − (1 × 2) = 15 + 2 − 2 = 15. Zaid: (1 × 5) + (7 × 1) − (2 × 2) = 5 + 7 − 4 = 8. 15 − 8 = 7.
2. Hamza = water-carrier, kid2 = stone-cutter, Idris = scribe.
Hamza is the water-carrier (Clue 2). kid2 is not the scribe (Clue 1) and the water job is taken, so kid2 is the stone-cutter. That leaves scribe for Idris.
3. Top layer = 5 blocks; 155 blocks in all.
80 → 40 → 20 → 10 → 5. Total: 80 + 40 + 20 + 10 + 5 = 155.
4. kid1 10 symbols, kid2 16.
Take the 6 extra off first: 26 − 6 = 20, then 20 ÷ 2 = 10 for kid1. kid2 = 10 + 6 = 16. Check: 10 + 16 = 26. ✓
5. 3 groups; 10 greetings per group.
15 ÷ 5 = 3 groups. In a group of 5 (A–E): AB, AC, AD, AE, BC, BD, BE, CD, CE, DE = 10 greetings.
