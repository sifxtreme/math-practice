Did you know? Grey whales make one of the longest migrations of any mammal — up to 20,000 km in a year.

SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Wildebeest cross the same rivers every year. Over a million of them move together in one great loop.

Bilal weighs one tracker at 3.6 g and a second at 1.85 g. What is their total weight, and how much heavier is the first than the second?
SHOW YOUR THINKING

Did you know? Scientists keep a tracker under 3% of a bird's body weight — about the same rule as a backpack for you.

Answer:
Did you know? Migration maps are built from millions of pings like these, stitched together into one line across the world.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa
1. 681 birds still flying.
128 × 7 = 896, then 896 − 215 = 681. Multiply first, subtract second — 128 − 215 is the give-away that the order slipped.
2. 18 crates.
154 ÷ 9 = 17 remainder 1. That 1 leftover bird still needs a crate, so round up to 18. “17” is the answer that ignores the question.
3. 54 butterflies per page.
168 + 264 = 432, then 432 ÷ 8 = 54. Add both halves of the day before splitting. Dividing 168 by 8 alone forgets the afternoon.
4. The 2/3 box.
Twenty-fourths: 2/3 = 16/24 and 5/8 = 15/24, so 2/3 is more. They are close on purpose — this one cannot be eyeballed.
5. Other side 11 m; perimeter 46 m.
132 ÷ 12 = 11 m (the missing side). Perimeter = 2 × (12 + 11) = 46 m. Given area, asked perimeter: divide for the side, don't halve.
Watch for: Cumulative — five different skills on one sheet, no scaffolds. #2 is the remainder-interpretation one: expect “17” and ask what happens to the last bird. #4 is deliberately close (16/24 vs 15/24) so the bigger bottom number can't be read as “more”. #5: halving 132 to get 66 is the classic slip — it must be a divide by the known side.
4th Grade — Yusuf
1. 1,222 birds.
47 × 26 = (47 × 20) + (47 × 6) = 940 + 282 = 1,222. Two-digit by two-digit: the second partial row shifts one column left.
2. 494 km per day, 3 km left over.
3,461 ÷ 7 = 494 remainder 3. Check: 494 × 7 + 3 = 3,461. ✓ The question asks for both numbers.
3. 13/30 of a tank left.
Thirtieths: 5/6 = 25/30 and 2/5 = 12/30. 25/30 − 12/30 = 13/30. Unlike denominators — straight across gives 3/1, which is more than a full tank and fails its own sense check.
4. Total 5.45 g; the first is heavier by 1.75 g.
Write 3.6 as 3.60 and line the points up. 3.60 + 1.85 = 5.45; 3.60 − 1.85 = 1.75. The padding has to be written, not just thought.
5. 1,365 pings — 151 full files, 6 left over.
Rate first: 2,184 ÷ 8 = 273 per hour. Then 273 × 5 = 1,365. Then 1,365 ÷ 9 = 151 r 6. Check: 151 × 9 + 6 = 1,365. ✓
Watch for: Cumulative, no scaffolds anywhere. #4 is the decimal-padding check — an answer of 5.45 with no 3.60 written down is not a pass on these sheets. #5: jumping straight to 2,184 × 5 skips the per-hour rate and is the most likely wrong turn. #2 and #5 both want the remainder reported, not dropped.
