🚣 Which Boat Is Farther Down the River?

New skill — comparing fractions with different bottoms
3rd Grade
Name:  Musa
Date:
HOW IT WORKS
Two boats race down the Nile. Boat A has finished 2/3 of the trip. Boat B has finished 3/5 of the trip. Which boat is farther along?
Step 1 — Notice the problem. Thirds and fifths are different-sized pieces. You can't just compare the tops.
Step 2 — Make the bottoms the same. Multiply the bottoms: 3 × 5 = 15.
Step 3 — Rewrite each one. 2/3 = 10/15 (× 5 top and bottom). 3/5 = 9/15 (× 3 top and bottom).
Step 4 — Compare the tops. 10/15 > 9/15, so Boat A (2/3) is farther along.
The trap: a bigger bottom number does not mean a bigger fraction. 1/8 is smaller than 1/3 — more pieces means each piece is smaller.

HOW IT WORKS
A riverboat covers 1/2 of its journey on the first day and 1/3 more on the second day. How much of the journey is done?
Step 1 — Notice the problem. Halves and thirds are different-sized pieces. You cannot add 1 + 1, because the pieces aren't the same size.
Step 2 — Find a size they can both become. Multiply the bottoms: 2 × 3 = 6. Cut everything into sixths.
Step 3 — Rewrite each fraction. 1/2 = 3/6 (multiply top and bottom by 3). 1/3 = 2/6 (multiply top and bottom by 2).
Step 4 — Now add. 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6. The bottom number does not change — you're just counting sixths.
The trap: 1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Never add the bottoms. Multiplying the two bottoms together always gives a size they share, even if it isn't the smallest one.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps + what to watch for
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa (comparing fractions)
Watch for: deciding a fraction is bigger because its bottom number is bigger. That's the whole misconception this sheet targets (#4 is built to catch it). If it shows up, draw two identical rivers and cut one into 3 pieces and one into 4 — the pieces of the one cut into 4 are visibly smaller.
1. Amir is farther across.
Bottom 8 (4 goes into 8). 3/4 = 6/8. Compare tops: 6 vs 5 → 6/8 > 5/8, so Amir (3/4).
2. The same amount.
1/2 = 3/6 (multiply top and bottom by 3). Both gauges show the same water — 3/6 is just 1/2 written in sixths.
3. The second stream (1/2) carries more.
Bottom 10. 2/5 = 4/10 and 1/2 = 5/10. 5/10 > 4/10, so the 1/2 stream carries more.
4. Sana (1/3) is the bigger fraction.
Bottom 12. 1/3 = 4/12 and 1/4 = 3/12. 4/12 > 3/12, so 1/3 > 1/4. The bottom got bigger but the fraction got smaller — that's the trap.
4th Grade — Yusuf (unlike denominators)
Watch for: adding the bottom numbers (1/4 + 2/5 = 3/9). If that happens, go back to the two-day boat picture — you can't count pieces of different sizes. Also watch for converting one fraction and forgetting to convert the other.
1. 13/20 of the river.
Common bottom 4 × 5 = 20. 1/4 = 5/20, 2/5 = 8/20. 5/20 + 8/20 = 13/20.
2. 5/8 of the dam's water.
8 already works (4 divides into 8). 1/4 = 2/8. 3/8 + 2/8 = 5/8. Good one to point out you don't always have to multiply the bottoms.
3. 27/20 miles = 1 7/20 miles — more than 1 mile.
Common bottom 4 × 5 = 20. 3/4 = 15/20, 3/5 = 12/20. 15/20 + 12/20 = 27/20 = 1 whole and 7/20. Since 27 > 20, it's more than a mile.
4. 7/12 of the reservoir.
Common bottom 12. 5/6 = 10/12, 1/4 = 3/12. 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12. Same procedure as adding — only the last step changes.
