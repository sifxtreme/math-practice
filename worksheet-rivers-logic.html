🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa
1. Z, W, X, Y.
W > X and Y < X puts them W, X, Y. Z > W goes on the front: Z, W, X, Y. Build the chain one clue at a time instead of guessing.
2. Tariq — barge. Nadia — ferry. Yusra — canoe.
Tariq is given. Nadia is not the canoe and the barge is taken, so Nadia has the ferry. Yusra gets what is left. The “not” clue does the work.
3. 80 cm.
1:00 → 2:00 is 60 minutes = four 15-minute steps. 5 → 10 → 20 → 40 → 80. Counting the doublings is the puzzle; 5 × 4 = 20 is the trap.
Both: stones 15 and 30. Neither: 16 stones.
Both = multiples of 15 → 15 and 30. Mossy (3, 6, 9…) = 10 stones, painted (5, 10, 15…) = 6, minus the 2 counted twice = 14 tagged, so 30 − 14 = 16 with neither.
Watch for: #3 is the one that gets missed — the trap is multiplying by 4 instead of doubling four times. On #4, expect 10 + 6 = 16 tagged; stones 15 and 30 get counted twice and must come back out. On #2, the “not” clue is easy to skip straight past.
4th Grade — Yusuf
1. Silver, Blue, Grey, Amber, Green.
Silver > Blue > Grey from the first and third clues; Green is below the Grey; the Amber sits between Green and Grey. The “between” clue only makes sense once the others are placed.
2. No — it proves nothing about Saturday.
Her statement is only about Friday's boats. A kayak on Saturday is neither proof nor disproof. The one thing that would disprove her: a Friday boat that was not a kayak. Accept any answer that separates “all Friday's were kayaks” from “every kayak is launched on Friday”.
3. 3:10 in the afternoon (15:10) local time.
7:20 + 6 h = 1:20 pm, + 50 min = 2:10 pm dock time. Local clocks are 1 hour ahead, so 3:10 pm. Do the elapsed time first, adjust the zone second.
4. 7 buoys.
240 ÷ 40 = 6 gaps, and buoys at both ends means 6 + 1 = 7. The classic fencepost error: 6 is the number of gaps, not buoys.
5. 10 handshakes.
Each of the 5 teams shakes 4 others = 20, but every handshake was counted from both sides, so 20 ÷ 2 = 10. Or add 4 + 3 + 2 + 1.
Watch for: #4 and #5 are both counting traps in opposite directions — #4 needs a plus one, #5 needs a divide by two. Expect 6 and 20. #2 has no number in it and is the most valuable one on the sheet: the reasoning written down is the answer, so a bare “no” is not finished.
