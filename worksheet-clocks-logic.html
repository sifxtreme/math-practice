Puzzles about keeping time
3rd Grade

1.
A kitchen clock runs 15 minutes slow. It shows 3:40. What is the real time?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Before railways, every town kept its own local time by the sun — noon in one town was not noon in the next.
2.
A tower bell strikes the hour: once at 1 o'clock, twice at 2 o'clock, and so on. How many strikes altogether from 1 o'clock through 6 o'clock?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? We split hours into 60 minutes because the Babylonians counted in sixties, more than 4,000 years ago.
3.
A clock chimes every 15 minutes. How many chimes are there from 2:00 to 4:00, counting the chimes at 2:00 and at 4:00 themselves?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? A sundial only works in sunshine, which is why water clocks were invented — they kept counting at night and indoors.
4.
Three friends arrive at the clock tower. Idris arrives before Salma. Yahya arrives after Salma. Who arrived first, and who arrived last?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The pendulum clock, invented in 1656, was such a leap that it cut a typical clock's daily error from minutes to seconds.

Puzzles about keeping time
4th Grade
Name:  kid2
Date:  
1.
You have a 4-minute hourglass and a 7-minute hourglass, and nothing else. How do you measure exactly 9 minutes? Write the steps with the times.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Working out longitude at sea needed a clock that stayed accurate on a rolling ship — the problem stood for decades.
2.
Three bells ring together at noon. One rings every 6 minutes, one every 8 minutes, one every 12 minutes. How many minutes until all three ring together again?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? An atomic clock is so steady it would drift less than a second over millions of years.
3.
A train arrives at 3:45. The journey took 1 hour 50 minutes, and before boarding he waited 25 minutes at the station. What time did he reach the station?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Railways forced countries onto standard time — trains cannot run to a timetable if every town keeps its own clock.
4.
At 4:20, what is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand? (Careful: by 4:20 the hour hand has already crept past the 4.)
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? A clock face is 360 degrees over 12 hours, so the hour hand moves just half a degree every minute.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — kid1
1. 3:55.
Slow means it hasn't caught up yet, so the real time is later: 3:40 + 15 minutes = 3:55. Ask which way before adding — that's the whole question.
2. 21 strikes.
1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21. Pair the ends to go faster: (1+6) + (2+5) + (3+4) = 7 + 7 + 7.
3. 9 chimes.
2:00 to 4:00 is 120 minutes, and 120 ÷ 15 = 8 gaps. Counting both ends means 9 chimes, not 8 — fenceposts, not fences.
4. Idris first, Yahya last.
Idris before Salma, and Salma before Yahya, chains into Idris → Salma → Yahya. So Idris first and Yahya last.
Watch for: #3 is the fencepost trap: 120 ÷ 15 = 8 gaps but 9 chimes. Expect 8. It's the same idea as the leftover-needs-a-box remainder work he's been doing all week — worth naming that out loud, because he'll see they're the same shape.
4th Grade — kid2
1. Yes — 9 minutes, using four flips.
Start both at 0. At 4 min the small one empties — flip it. At 7 min the big one empties — flip it. At 8 min the small one empties again; the big one has now been running 1 minute, so it holds exactly 1 minute of sand in the bottom. Flip the big one and it runs out at 9 minutes. The idea: a part-used glass measures the time it has already run.
2. 24 minutes.
Find the first number 6, 8 and 12 all divide into. Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24. 24 is divisible by 6 and 12 too, so 24 minutes.
3. 1:30.
Work backwards from the arrival. 3:45 − 1 h 50 min = 1:55 departure. Then 1:55 − 25 min = 1:30. Undo the journey first, then the wait.
4. 10 degrees.
Minute hand: 20 min × 6° = 120°. Hour hand: 4 × 30° = 120°, plus 20 min × 0.5° = 10°, so 130°. Difference = 10°. Answering 20° means forgetting the hour hand drifts.
Watch for: #1 is genuinely hard and #4 catches almost everyone at 20°. Neither is a fluency problem, so don't let a miss here read as a bad day — note them as puzzles attempted, not skills failed. If he lands the hourglass unaided, that's the strongest reasoning result of the week.
