1.
Three students charted stars one night: kid1, Idris, and Bilal. Together they charted 14 stars. Idris charted 6 and Bilal charted 3. How many did kid1 chart? Whose number was in the middle?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Al-Sufi drew a star atlas in the year 964 that described the Andromeda Galaxy — over 900 years before telescopes could show what it was.
2.
An astrolabe is engraved with a ring of numbers: 5, 10, 15, 20, ___, ___ . What are the next two, and what is the rule?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? An astrolabe was a handheld computer made of brass. It could tell you the time, find your direction, and locate a star.
3.
Aisha counted 11 stars through a sighting tube. She saw 3 more bright ones than dim ones. How many bright and how many dim? (Hint: try pairs that add to 11.)
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Many star names we still use are Arabic — Betelgeuse, Rigel, Aldebaran, Vega.
4.
Four scholars finished copying a manuscript: Hamza, Layla, Omar, and Zayd. Hamza finished 1st. Zayd finished last. Layla finished ahead of Omar. List all four in order.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Paper reached Baghdad in the 700s, and books became cheap enough that the city had over 100 bookshops.
5.
kid1 is making an observation kit with one tool — astrolabe, quadrant, or sundial — and one notebook, leather or cloth. How many different kits can he make? List them.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? A quadrant measures the angle between the horizon and a star, which tells you how far north or south you are.

1.
In a stargazing game, spotting a planet earns 5 points, a bright star earns 2, and a cloudy night costs 3. kid2 spots 3 planets and 4 bright stars but loses 2 nights to clouds. Zaid spots 2 planets, 6 bright stars, and loses 1 night. Who has more, and by how much?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Only five planets can be seen without a telescope: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn.
2.
Three astronomers — kid2, Idris, and Hamza — each have one job: measuring, recording, and drawing. Clue 1: kid2 is not the recorder. Clue 2: Hamza is the artist who draws. Who does what?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Al-Battani measured the length of a year as 365 days, 5 hours, 46 minutes, 24 seconds — off by only about 2 minutes.
3.
An observatory starts with 96 star charts. Each year, half of them are recopied onto fresh paper and the old ones retired. After 4 years, how many of the original charts are left? How many were retired in all?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The Maragheh observatory in Persia had a library of 40,000 books and a staff of astronomers from as far away as China.
4.
Two brothers recorded moon sightings all month. Together they recorded 28. kid2 recorded 8 more than kid1. How many did each record?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The Islamic calendar follows the moon, so its months shift about 11 days earlier each solar year.
5.
A school of 18 students is split into equal groups of 6 to share telescopes. How many groups? Within one group, each student compares notes with every other student exactly once. How many comparisons happen in that group?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? The word 'algebra' comes from al-jabr, the title of a book written in Baghdad around 820.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — reasoning included
Coach
3rd Grade — kid1
1. kid1 charted 5; kid1 is the middle.
14 − 6 − 3 = 5. In order: Bilal 3, kid1 5, Idris 6 → kid1 is in the middle.
2. 25 and 30. Rule: add 5.
Each number goes up by 5. 20 + 5 = 25, 25 + 5 = 30.
3. 7 bright, 4 dim.
Adds to 11, differs by 3. 7 + 4 = 11 and 7 − 4 = 3. So 7 bright, 4 dim.
4. Hamza, Layla, Omar, Zayd.
Hamza 1st, Zayd 4th. Layla ahead of Omar → Layla 2nd, Omar 3rd.
5. 6 kits.
3 tools × 2 notebooks = 6. (astrolabe-leather, astrolabe-cloth, quadrant-leather, quadrant-cloth, sundial-leather, sundial-cloth.)
4th Grade — kid2
1. kid2 17, Zaid 19. Zaid leads by 2.
kid2: (3×5) + (4×2) − (2×3) = 15 + 8 − 6 = 17. Zaid: (2×5) + (6×2) − (1×3) = 10 + 12 − 3 = 19. 19 − 17 = 2.
2. Hamza draws, kid2 measures, Idris records.
Hamza draws (Clue 2). kid2 isn't the recorder (Clue 1) and drawing is taken → kid2 measures. Idris records.
3. 6 charts left; 90 retired.
96 → 48 → 24 → 12 → 6 after 4 years. Retired: 96 − 6 = 90.
4. kid1 10, kid2 18.
Take the 8 off first: 28 − 8 = 20, then 20 ÷ 2 = 10 for kid1. kid2 = 10 + 8 = 18. Check: 10 + 18 = 28. ✓
5. 3 groups; 15 comparisons per group.
18 ÷ 6 = 3 groups. In a group of 6 (A–F): 5+4+3+2+1 = 15 pairs.
