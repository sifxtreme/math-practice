🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa
1. D, A, B, C.
A > B and C < B puts them A, B, C. D > A goes on the front: D, A, B, C. Build the chain one clue at a time instead of guessing.
2. Bilal — Top Lock. Idris — Middle Lock. Salma — Bottom Lock.
Bilal is given. Idris is not the Bottom and the Top is taken, so Idris has the Middle. Salma gets what is left. The "not" clue does the work.
3. 32 cm.
9:00 → 10:00 is 60 minutes = three 20-minute steps. 4 → 8 → 16 → 32. Counting the doublings is the puzzle; 4 × 3 = 12 is the trap.
Both: posts 12 and 24. Neither: 16 posts.
Both = multiples of 12 → 12 and 24. Red (4, 8, 12…) = 6 posts, bollards (6, 12, 18, 24) = 4, minus the 2 counted twice = 8 tagged, so 24 − 8 = 16 with neither.
Watch for: #3 is the one that gets missed — the trap is multiplying by 3 instead of doubling three times. On #4, expect 6 + 4 = 10 tagged; posts 12 and 24 get counted twice and must come back out. On #2, the "not" clue is easy to skip straight past.
4th Grade — Yusuf
1. Grand, Delta, Reach, Mill, Cut.
Grand > Delta > Reach from the first and third clues; the Cut is below the Reach; the Mill sits between the Cut and the Reach. The "between" clue only makes sense once the others are placed.
2. No — it proves nothing about Tuesday.
Her statement is only about Monday's boats. A toll paid on Tuesday is neither proof nor disproof. The one thing that would disprove her: a Monday boat that did not pay. Accept any answer that separates "all Monday's boats paid" from "every boat that pays is a Monday boat".
3. 1:25 in the afternoon (13:25) local time.
8:40 + 5 h = 1:40 pm, + 45 min = 2:25 pm start time. Local clocks are 1 hour behind, so 1:25 pm. Do the elapsed time first, adjust the zone second.
4. 10 gates.
180 ÷ 20 = 9 gaps, and gates at both ends means 9 + 1 = 10. The classic fencepost error: 9 is the number of gaps, not gates.
5. 15 handshakes.
Each of the 6 crews shakes 5 others = 30, but every handshake was counted from both sides, so 30 ÷ 2 = 15. Or add 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1.
Watch for: #4 and #5 are both counting traps in opposite directions — #4 needs a plus one, #5 needs a divide by two. Expect 9 and 30. #2 has no number in it and is the most valuable one on the sheet: the reasoning written down is the answer, so a bare "no" is not finished.
