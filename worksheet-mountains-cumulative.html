Answer:

SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Every kilogram carried above 7,000 m costs roughly twice the effort it would at sea level.

SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Climbers often "climb high, sleep low" on purpose — going up to work the body, coming down to recover.

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — kid1
1. Other side 9 m; perimeter 30 m.
54 ÷ 6 = 9 m. Perimeter = 6 + 9 + 6 + 9 = 30 m.
2. 10 trips.
77 ÷ 8 = 9 remainder 5. The last 5 climbers still need a ride: 10 trips.
3. The 2/3 team.
24ths: 5/8 = 15/24 and 2/3 = 16/24. 16 beats 15, so 2/3 is further — only just.
4. 652 metres.
174 × 5 = 870 m, then 870 − 218 = 652 m.
5. 7 packs each.
9 × 6 = 54, then 54 − 12 = 42, then 42 ÷ 6 = 7. Three steps, and the order matters.
Watch for: This is the Locked test. All five of his pinned skills are here, shuffled, with nothing naming the method — #2 is the remainder problem sitting between a perimeter question and a fraction comparison. Getting #2 right here is worth more than four right on a sheet where every problem was division. Mark Locked only if he chose the method himself.
4th Grade — kid2
1. 1,786 packs.
38 × 47 = (38 × 40) + (38 × 7) = 1,520 + 266 = 1,786.
2. 7/12 of the way down.
12ths: 5/6 = 10/12 and 1/4 = 3/12. 10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12.
3. 602 metres a day, remainder 1.
4,215 ÷ 7 = 602 remainder 1. Check: 602 × 7 = 4,214, plus 1 = 4,215. ✓
4. 5.75 kg.
9.40 − 3.65 = 5.75 kg. Pad the 9.4 to 9.40 first.
5. 1,775 metres.
320 × 7 = 2,240 m, then 2,240 − 465 = 1,775 m.
Watch for: Same test for kid2 — #2 is unlike denominators with no heading announcing it. Watch whether he still reaches for a common denominator when the problem doesn't look like a fractions worksheet.
