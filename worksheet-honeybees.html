🐝 The Hive

Word problems from the beekeeper's yard
3rd Grade

1.
A beekeeper has 95 frames to move. Each crate holds 6 frames. How many crates are completely full, and how many crates does she need for all of them?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Honeycomb cells are hexagons because that shape stores the most honey using the least wax.
2.
A hive gains 145 bees a day for 4 days. Over the same period 96 bees do not return. What is the change in the hive's population?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? A single worker bee makes about one twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in her whole life.
3.
There are 8 hives with 9 frames in each. The frames are shared equally between 3 beekeepers. How many frames does each beekeeper carry?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Bees tell each other where flowers are by dancing in a figure of eight — the angle points the way.
4.
One jar is 1/2 full of honey. Another is 4/10 full. Which jar has more honey?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Honey found sealed in Egyptian tombs was still perfectly good to eat thousands of years later.
5.
A rectangular bee garden covers 63 square metres. One side is 9 m. Find the other side and the perimeter.
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Bees can see ultraviolet light, so many flowers carry glowing patterns that guide them in like runway lights.

Word problems from the beekeeper's yard
4th Grade
Name:  kid2
Date:  
1.
A frame is 5/6 filled with honey. The beekeeper takes 3/8 of a frame's worth. What fraction of the frame is still full?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? A strong hive can hold more than 50,000 bees in summer, and the queen may lay 2,000 eggs a day.
2.
A harvest of 2,375 grams of honey is poured equally into 8 jars. How much goes in each jar, and how many grams are left over?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Bees visit about two million flowers to make a single pound of honey.
3.
One hive produced 2.4 kg of honey and another produced 3.75 kg. What is the total?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Bees keep the middle of the hive at about 35 °C all year by shivering to warm it and fanning to cool it.
4.
An apiary has 34 hives, each holding 56 frames. How many frames altogether?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Beekeepers use smoke because it masks the alarm signal bees release, so the hive stays calm.
5.
A hive yields 15 kg of honey a week for 12 weeks. The beekeeper sells 45.5 kg at the market. How much is left?
SHOW YOUR THINKING
Answer:
Did you know? Honey never spoils. Its sugar draws water out of any bacteria that lands in it.

🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — kid1
1. 15 full crates; 16 crates in total.
95 ÷ 6 = 15 remainder 5. The last 5 frames still need a crate: 16.
2. Up by 484 bees.
145 × 4 = 580 gained, then 580 − 96 = 484.
3. 24 frames each.
8 × 9 = 72 frames, then 72 ÷ 3 = 24.
4. The 1/2 jar.
Tenths: 1/2 = 5/10, and 5/10 is more than 4/10.
5. Other side 7 m; perimeter 32 m.
63 ÷ 9 = 7 m. Perimeter = 9 + 7 + 9 + 7 = 32 m.
Watch for: Fourth day on remainders. If #1 comes back as 16 crates first time, with no prompting, that is two clean runs in a row — check the log, because the step-up rule may now be satisfied for this skill.
4th Grade — kid2
1. 11/24 of the frame.
24ths: 5/6 = 20/24 and 3/8 = 9/24. 20/24 − 9/24 = 11/24.
2. 296 grams per jar, 7 grams left over.
2,375 ÷ 8 = 296 remainder 7. Check: 296 × 8 = 2,368, plus 7 = 2,375. ✓
3. 6.15 kg.
Pad the shorter one: 2.40 + 3.75 = 6.15 kg.
4. 1,904 frames.
34 × 56 = (34 × 50) + (34 × 6) = 1,700 + 204 = 1,904.
5. 134.5 kg.
15 × 12 = 180 kg, then 180 − 45.5 = 134.5 kg.
Watch for: #1 and the Jul 30 sheet's #1 are the same skill in opposite directions (add vs subtract). Getting the add right and the subtract wrong usually means he is finding the common denominator fine but slipping on the borrow.
