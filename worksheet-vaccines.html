🔑 Answer Key

For the grown-ups — worked steps included
Coach
3rd Grade — Musa
1. 800 doses.
128 × 7 = 896 handed out over the week, then 896 − 96 = 800 actually used. Multiply first, subtract second.
2. 9 full boxes, 3 left over — but 10 boxes to carry them all.
75 ÷ 8 = 9 remainder 3. Those 3 spare vials still have to travel, so one more box: 10.
3. 55 doses left.
6 × 24 = 144 set out, then 144 − 89 = 55.
4. The 2/3 box.
Twenty-fourths: 5/8 = 15/24 and 2/3 = 16/24. So 2/3 holds more, by one twenty-fourth — and note it is the fraction with the smaller numbers this time.
5. Other side 9 cm; area 126 square cm.
Halve first: 46 ÷ 2 = 23, one long side plus one short. Then 23 − 14 = 9 cm. Area = 14 × 9 = 126 square cm.
Watch for: Day three on missing sides, scaffolding all gone — #5 is yesterday's guided problem alone. Halving is the whole test: 32 means he never halved. On #2 expect "9 boxes", one step short. On #4 he may pick 5/8 for its bigger numbers.
4th Grade — Yusuf
1. 11/12 of the box.
Twelfths: 1/6 = 2/12 and 3/4 = 9/12, so 2/12 + 9/12 = 11/12. Add the tops, keep the bottom. Both had to change here — neither bottom was already 12.
2. 11/24 of the store.
Twenty-fourths: 5/6 = 20/24 and 3/8 = 9/24. 20/24 − 9/24 = 11/24. The bottom number never changes once you have it.
3. 1,620 doses.
36 × 45. Split it: (36 × 40) + (36 × 5) = 1,440 + 180 = 1,620.
4. 7.85 mL drawn; 4.65 mL left.
Pad and line up the points. 3.60 + 4.25 = 7.85. Then 12.50 − 7.85 = 4.65 mL. Padding matters most in the subtraction: 12.5 read as 12.05 gives 4.20.
5. 3,344 people — 371 complete folders, 5 left over.
Find one day first: 1,824 ÷ 6 = 304 a day. Then 304 × 11 = 3,344. Finally 3,344 ÷ 9 = 371 remainder 5. Check: 3,339 + 5 = 3,344. ✓
Watch for: Second guided day back on unlike denominators. #1 needs both fractions converted, which yesterday's did not. #2 is the real read — no scaffold, and a subtraction. Working straight across gives 2/2; ask whether the store can be empty when it started 5/6 full. On #5, jumping to 1,824 × 11 is yesterday's miss repeating.
